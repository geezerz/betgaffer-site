It's a bet on goals at both ends, not on who wins.

Many bookmakers call the same bet "both teams to score" (BTTS): Yes is GG, No is NG.

How it settles

These are example scores, not real matches.

Final scoreGGNG
1–1WonLost
2–1WonLost
3–0LostWon
0–0LostWon

The winner doesn't matter, only whether both sides scored. A 1–1 draw wins GG; a 4–0 win loses it.

Most bookmakers settle it on the score at the end of regular time, including added time, so extra time and penalties don't count. Rules differ by bookmaker, so check yours.

Combined bets

SportyBet also offers GG/NG combined with Over/Under, such as GG and Over 2.5. Both parts have to come true for the bet to win.

Rules can change, so check SportyBet's own rules.

From SportyBet's football rules, checked {{checked_long}}:

  • "Predict whether both teams will score in the match at regular time."
  • "*Yes (GG both teams score) *No (NG any team not to score)."
  • "Over/Under & GG/NG: Predict whether the total number of goals at regular time is over/under a given line and if both teams will score."

Easy to get wrong

  • Lots of goals can still lose GG. 4–0 has four goals, but only one team scored.
  • GG and Over 2.5 aren't the same bet. 1–1 wins GG but loses Over 2.5.
  • A goalless draw is NG. 0–0 wins NG.
